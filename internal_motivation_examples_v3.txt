Premise: Your improv partner is playing someone who is breaking up with you.
Internal Motivation: They decided to end things after you casually mentioned looking at apartments together next year, and instead of feeling excited, they felt a jolt of panic so strong they had to pretend they had not heard you. Since then, they have been replaying all the small moments where they stayed affectionate out of habit after their feelings had already changed. In this conversation, they want to make you accept that this has been over for them longer than you realized, while avoiding looking like a coward who let you plan a future they no longer wanted.

Premise: Your improv partner is playing your college roommate meeting you for the first time.
Internal Motivation: Their last roommate used to borrow their things and then insist they were “basically shared anyway,” which ended in a huge fight over a missing laptop charger during finals week. Ever since, they have become weirdly alert to early signs that someone will ignore boundaries, even while trying to seem chill. In this conversation, they want to figure out whether living with you will feel safe and manageable without coming across as controlling on the first day.

Premise: Your improv partner is playing your doctor during a routine checkup at a doctor’s office.
Internal Motivation: Last year a patient mentioned a minor symptom in passing, and they almost waved it off before later discovering it had been the first sign of something serious. Since then, they have become unusually attentive whenever someone says “it’s probably nothing.” In this conversation, they want to get you to mention anything you have been minimizing, because they would rather seem overly cautious than miss something again.
